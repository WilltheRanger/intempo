One line of music reads best — that is what the prompt asks for. HEIC straight off an iPhone is fine.

Drag a box around one staff. The prompt reads a single line of sheet music. Give it a whole page and it has to keep its place across five of them, which is where transcriptions come back with measures repeated or invented. One staff at a time is not a limitation to work around — it is what the thing is built to do.

Pipeline

Everything except the reads is arithmetic that runs here, in this page: the staff is located, the pitch grid drawn and the slices cut without a model and without a request. The model is asked one question, once per slice — what notes are in this picture. It never sees the whole page, never locates the staff, and never computes a coordinate. The trace marks which side each step ran on.

The pitch grid is off because this browser cannot open a HEIC, and it has to measure the staff in the image to draw one. Open the bench in Safari, or set the iPhone to Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible so it shoots JPEG. The other stages still run.

Each read is a separate billed request, so two reads cost twice and a repair pass costs once more. Stage 1 and the capture gate are free — they run here.

Thinking is off by default because gemini_provider.py turns it off, on the reasoning that “for OCR we don’t need internal reasoning.” That is worth testing rather than assuming — reading dense notation may be exactly where reasoning earns its cost, and it is the one setting that differs most between Flash, where it was tuned, and Pro, where it was not. Thinking tokens are billed as output.

Ground truth — optional, and the only way to know

Type the rhythm of what is actually on the page and every read gets scored against it. Rhythm only — the analysis reads note.pitch in exactly one place, to ask whether a note is a rest, and builds its whole timeline from durations. So this is about half the typing of a real transcription and covers everything the product consumes.

w h q e s t = whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth, thirty-second. Add . to dot it, r to make it a rest. Separate measures with |. So a bar of four quarters then two halves is q q q q | h h, and a dotted quarter, eighth and quarter rest is q. e qr.

Your key is sent to the model provider and nowhere else — this page has no backend. It is kept in this browser’s localStorage so you don’t retype it, and never leaves the machine except in the request you just made. Costs are real: roughly $0.005 a page on Flash, $0.03 on Sonnet.

Photographs are re-encoded before they are sent: HEIC becomes JPEG, anything over 2000px is scaled down, and EXIF rotation is applied to the pixels so a portrait shot doesn’t arrive sideways. Stage 1b is the exception — it reads the file at full resolution, because it re-renders slices of it and cannot enlarge detail that was already discarded. A 12-megapixel phone photo goes from about 3 MB to 30 KB, which the models would have downsampled anyway and which you would otherwise have been billed for. HEIC needs Safari to convert; on Chrome or Firefox it is passed to Gemini as-is, which accepts the format — Claude does not.
